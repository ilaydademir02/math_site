<Definition title="Compact metric space">

A metric space $(X, d)$ is **compact** if every open cover of $X$ admits a finite subcover. Equivalently, $(X,d)$ is compact if and only if it is complete and totally bounded.

</Definition>

<Theorem title="Extreme Value Theorem">

Let $X$ be a compact metric space and let $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ be continuous. Then $f$ attains a maximum and a minimum on $X$.

</Theorem>

<Proof>

Since $f$ is continuous and $X$ is compact, the image $f(X) \subset \mathbb{R}$ is compact, hence closed and bounded. A closed and bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}$ contains its supremum and infimum, so there exist $x_1, x_2 \in X$ with

$$
f(x_1) = \sup_{x \in X} f(x), \qquad f(x_2) = \inf_{x \in X} f(x).
$$

</Proof>

<Example title="A non-example">

The function $f(x) = 1/x$ on $(0,1)$ is continuous but attains neither a maximum nor a minimum, since $(0,1)$ is not compact.

</Example>
